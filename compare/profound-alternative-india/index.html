Profound Alternative for India — What Indian Teams Actually Need

Profound is built for US enterprise teams. If you are an Indian brand targeting Indian buyers — where Gemini leads ChatGPT — the platform and pricing gaps matter. Here is the honest breakdown.

What Profound does — and who it is built for

Profound is an AI visibility analytics platform that monitors how brands appear in AI responses. It tracks citation rates across ChatGPT and Perplexity, shows share-of-voice trends over time, and provides research-grade data for enterprise marketing teams. It is one of the more credible tools in the category and is used by US-based enterprise teams that need to report AI visibility metrics to CMOs and boards.

The product is designed for US buyers: USD pricing, US-market query sets, US-platform weighting (ChatGPT and Perplexity dominate in the US), and enterprise contracts that start above what most Indian startups or agencies would budget for GEO tooling. That is not a criticism of Profound as a product. It is an honest description of who the product is for.

Where Profound falls short for Indian teams

Three structural gaps make Profound a poor fit for Indian brands targeting Indian buyers.

Gemini is not treated as a primary platform. In India, Gemini leads the chatbot market with 52% download share — driven by Android device dominance. A GEO monitoring tool that treats Gemini as secondary misses the majority of the Indian AI audience. In the US, ChatGPT leads. The platform weighting difference is significant, and a tool calibrated for the US market will generate misleading data for an Indian brand.

Trust signal evaluation is US-centric. Profound evaluates brand visibility against trust signals that matter in the US: Reddit community volume, G2 reviews, Amazon presence, and Trustpilot. For Indian buyers, the relevant trust signals are different: INR pricing transparency, NASSCOM certification, Google Reviews and Maps ratings, and editorial coverage in The Economic Times, Mint, YourStory, and Inc42. A monitoring tool that does not evaluate these signals gives Indian brands an incomplete picture of why they are or are not being cited.

Pricing is USD and enterprise-tier. Profound's pricing is designed for US enterprise budgets. For an Indian startup or agency team working with a GEO budget of Rs 2,000 to Rs 8,000 per month, the price gap is not bridgeable by intent — it is structural. Most Indian teams need a tool that charges in a range that makes sense for Indian market economics.

The core issue with using a US tool for India: Citation rate data is only meaningful if the platform queries match how Indian buyers actually search. A tool testing "best project management software" will return different results than one testing "best project management tool for Indian SME under Rs 5000 per user per month." Both measure the same brand — but only one produces actionable insight for the Indian market.

What Indian teams need from a GEO tool in 2026

Based on my experience working with Indian brands on AI visibility, the minimum viable GEO tool for an Indian team targeting Indian buyers needs five things:

  • Gemini monitoring as a primary platform — not an afterthought bolted onto a ChatGPT-first product
  • India-market buying queries — INR budget constraints, Indian compliance questions, Indian ERP integration queries
  • Indian trust signal evaluation — INR pricing transparency, NASSCOM or sector certification, Google Reviews, Indian editorial mentions
  • Buying factor gap diagnosis — not just a citation score, but an explanation of which content gaps are causing the brand to be skipped
  • Pricing that works for Indian budgets — not USD enterprise contracts

A monitoring-only tool that gives you a citation rate without a diagnosis of why your brand is or is not cited does not give you an action. GEO improvement requires understanding which specific buying factors your content is missing — and generating the content to address them.

When Profound might still make sense for an Indian team

Profound is worth considering if your Indian team is primarily selling to US or European buyers — in which case US-centric platform weighting and trust signal evaluation are appropriate. A large Indian enterprise or agency that manages US-market clients with enterprise budgets may find Profound's research-grade data and reporting features valuable. For brands selling to Indian buyers through Indian channels, Profound's structural limitations for the Indian market outweigh its strengths for that specific use case.
