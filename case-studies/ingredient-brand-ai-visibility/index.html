Skincare

How a minimalist ingredient brand achieved high AI visibility with minimal content

Tested on 4 AI platforms Indian D2C brand Brand anonymised
81
AI visibility score / 100

Situation

A science-backed skincare brand that markets itself on ingredient transparency and formulation quality was tested for AI visibility. Unlike several peers in the cohort, this brand had a relatively lean content marketing footprint but tested in the top quartile across all four AI platforms.

What Was Tested

Queries tested covered ingredient-specific searches (niacinamide serums, retinol products, SPF recommendations) as well as general skincare recommendation queries. The brand was tested across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews.

All testing used Jeevan AI’s standard query set. Results reflect a specific point in time and may vary across sessions. This case study uses hedged language throughout to reflect the indirect nature of AI visibility measurement.

Platform Breakdown

ChatGPT cited

Cited consistently. Ingredient specificity and strong entity presence in training data appeared to be key factors.

Perplexity cited

Cited frequently. Product pages with structured ingredient data appeared to be retrieved reliably.

Gemini cited

Cited in most queries. Strong Google entity presence and Knowledge Panel coverage appeared to contribute.

Google AI Overviews cited

Appeared in triggered queries. Structured FAQ and ingredient content on the website appeared to be factors.

What the Testing Suggested

  • In testing, the brand was cited on all 4 platforms, placing it in the top quartile of the benchmark cohort.
  • Tested queries suggest that ingredient-level specificity on product pages may have helped AI models form a clear entity understanding of what the brand offers.
  • ChatGPT citations in ingredient-specific queries were notably consistent, which in testing appeared to correlate with the brand's presence in independent ingredient-focused editorial content.
  • Perplexity citations were strong, likely aided by the brand's structured product pages and strong organic rankings for ingredient queries.
  • The pattern suggests that a focused, well-structured content approach may outperform high-volume content if the structured signals are strong.

Signals That Appeared to Matter

  • Ingredient-level structured data on product pages
  • Product schema with composition and use-case information
  • Independent editorial coverage in ingredient-focused publications
  • Wikidata entity with ingredient and formulation information
  • FAQ schema covering ingredient questions
  • Consistent entity information across all owned and third-party pages

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a brand with less content outperform brands with more content in AI visibility?
In testing, yes. Content volume alone does not appear to be the primary driver of AI visibility. Structured signals, entity clarity and independent corroboration appeared to matter more in tested queries. A brand with 20 well-structured pages and strong entity coverage may outperform one with 200 pages and weak entity signals.
Why does ingredient specificity help AI visibility?
AI models appear to reward specificity because specific claims are easier to corroborate across multiple independent sources. A brand known for a specific ingredient such as niacinamide may be cited more reliably than a brand with a broad but vague positioning, because independent sources such as dermatology publications and ingredient guides are more likely to reference it consistently.
What is product schema and how does it help?
Product schema is structured data markup that tells search engines and AI crawlers specific facts about a product, such as ingredients, intended use, price range and reviews. In testing, brands with complete product schema appeared to have stronger AI visibility, possibly because the structured data reduces ambiguity about what the brand offers.
