A hair loss brand with strong SEO but weak AI presence: what the gap revealed

Tested on 4 AI platforms Indian D2C brand Brand anonymised
68
AI visibility score / 100

Situation

A hair loss brand with a clinical positioning and good organic search rankings was tested for AI visibility. Despite high keyword rankings for category queries, the brand tested below the median score in the Jeevan AI benchmark cohort. This suggested that SEO performance does not directly translate to AI visibility, particularly in health-adjacent categories.

What Was Tested

Queries tested covered hair loss causes, treatment options, ingredient efficacy and brand comparisons. The brand had strong content around its own products but limited mentions in independent clinical or editorial sources.

All testing used Jeevan AI’s standard query set. Results reflect a specific point in time and may vary across sessions. This case study uses hedged language throughout to reflect the indirect nature of AI visibility measurement.

Platform Breakdown

ChatGPT not cited

Not cited in tested queries. Clinical claims without third-party corroboration may reduce citation likelihood on this platform.

Perplexity cited

Cited in some queries, likely due to SEO rankings feeding into live retrieval. Citation context was limited.

Gemini not cited

Not cited in tested queries. Low Knowledge Graph entity coverage appeared to be a factor.

Google AI Overviews cited

Appeared in one triggered query. Structured FAQ content on the brand website appeared to contribute.

What the Testing Suggested

  • In testing, the brand appeared on 2 of 4 platforms, which is below median for the health and wellness category.
  • ChatGPT did not cite the brand in any tested queries, which in testing appeared to correlate with an absence of peer-reviewed or independent editorial sources discussing its efficacy.
  • Perplexity cited the brand in queries where it ranked highly in organic search, suggesting that its live retrieval layer picked up the brand from search results even when AI training data may not have included it.
  • Gemini did not include the brand, which in testing appeared linked to a low entity score in Google's Knowledge Graph.
  • The pattern suggests that health-adjacent brands may need independent clinical validation or editorial corroboration to perform well on training-data-dependent platforms like ChatGPT.

Signals That Appeared to Matter

  • Independent editorial coverage in health and wellness publications
  • Clinical study citations or research references on brand pages
  • Wikidata entry with product and founder information
  • FAQ schema covering common questions about the product category
  • Google Knowledge Panel or Knowledge Graph entity presence
  • Third-party review coverage on independent platforms

Frequently Asked Questions

Why would a brand rank well in SEO but poorly in AI visibility?
SEO ranking reflects how well a page is optimised for search engine crawling and ranking algorithms. AI visibility depends on whether AI models have encountered reliable, consistent information about the brand across many independent sources. A brand can rank highly through technical SEO while having limited AI-friendly entity signals.
Do clinical claims help AI visibility for health brands?
In testing, clinical claims that are supported by independent sources, such as published studies or editorial coverage in health publications, may help. Claims that appear only on brand-owned pages appeared less impactful in tested queries on platforms like ChatGPT.
What is the difference between Perplexity visibility and ChatGPT visibility?
Perplexity retrieves live web content when answering queries, so brands with strong SEO rankings may appear even if they are absent from ChatGPT's training data. ChatGPT relies primarily on its training data and any retrieval tools in the current session. The two platforms can therefore give very different visibility outcomes for the same brand.
