What Is Vector Search?

A definition of Vector Search, how it differs from keyword search, and why semantic similarity is the mechanism by which AI systems retrieve relevant brand content.

Definition

Vector Search is a retrieval method in which content and queries are represented as numerical vectors (embeddings) that capture their semantic meaning, and similarity is measured by the geometric distance between those vectors, allowing AI systems to find semantically relevant content rather than just keyword-matched content.

Vector Search in Plain Terms

Keyword search is a string matching operation: does this document contain these words? Vector search is a meaning matching operation: does this document discuss something semantically close to what the user is asking about?

The mechanism is mathematical. Each piece of content is converted into a vector (a list of hundreds or thousands of numbers) by an embedding model that encodes its meaning. When a query arrives, it is also embedded into a vector. Vector search finds the content vectors that are geometrically closest to the query vector in that high-dimensional space, returning the semantically most similar content.

AI systems that use RAG often rely on vector search for the retrieval step. This means that how well your content's meaning aligns with buyer queries, in semantic terms, affects how often it is retrieved and potentially cited.

Implications for Brand Content Strategy

Because vector search retrieves by semantic similarity, the following content practices tend to improve retrieval performance:

  • Comprehensive topic coverage: Content that addresses a topic thoroughly, covering its dimensions, use cases, and related concepts, produces richer semantic vectors and is more likely to be retrieved for the full range of related queries.
  • Natural language: Write the way buyers talk. Conversational, complete sentences encode meaning more richly than fragmented keyword lists.
  • Concept coverage: Include related concepts, synonyms, and related use cases naturally in content. A page about "CRM software" that also covers "customer relationship management," "sales pipeline tracking," and "deal management" will retrieve for a broader range of semantically related queries.
  • Topical depth over breadth: A deep, thorough page on a single topic tends to produce a stronger semantic signal than a shallow page covering many topics loosely.

Why Understanding Vector Search Improves AI Visibility

Teams that understand vector retrieval shift their content strategy away from keyword frequency toward topical authority and semantic completeness. This is aligned with best practices for AI visibility generally: comprehensive, well-structured content that accurately addresses buyer problems performs well in both vector retrieval and traditional ranked retrieval. The conceptual shift is from "which keywords should I include?" to "does this content fully address the semantic space my buyers are asking about?"

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Vector Search?

Vector Search retrieves content based on semantic meaning by comparing mathematical vector representations (embeddings) of the content and the query, finding the most semantically similar content even if it does not contain the exact query keywords.

Does Vector Search mean keywords no longer matter?

Keywords still matter for traditional search and as entry points to semantic meaning. But in vector search, keyword density matters less than semantic completeness and topical depth. Content that comprehensively covers a topic semantically will tend to be retrieved for a wider range of related queries.

Which AI systems use Vector Search?

Most enterprise RAG applications use vector search for their retrieval step. Consumer AI platforms like Perplexity use hybrid retrieval (combining traditional and semantic search). Understanding vector search helps brands optimize content for the full range of retrieval mechanisms.

How do I know if my content is performing well in vector retrieval?

The proxy signals are: being cited in AI responses for semantically related queries (not just exact-match queries), high citation rate across a broad range of queries in your category, and strong performance in Perplexity (which uses semantic retrieval heavily).
