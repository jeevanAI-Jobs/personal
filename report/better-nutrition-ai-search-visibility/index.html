What AI engines see today

Better Nutrition is India's first biofortified atta brand, growing 2X more zinc, iron, and calcium naturally into the grain rather than spraying nutrients on after harvest. The brand tests every batch for 270+ pesticides, 10+ heavy metals, and 20+ nutrients via XRF testing at the farm gate. PV Sindhu is both ambassador and investor. Why this score matters
Better Nutrition has positioned itself in one of the most structurally important food categories in India: everyday staples like atta, rice, and dal that every household buys repeatedly. The biofortification angle is genuinely differentiated from both organic (purity without density) and fortified (artificial spray-on nutrients). The brand's own homepage articulates this distinction clearly. The AI visibility challenge is that the buyer who types healthy atta India or nutritious wheat flour India into an AI engine is likely to get recommendations based on general nutrition content rather than biofortification-specific content. Better Nutrition's lab report transparency, named founders with strong institutional backgrounds, 2.5 lakh family trust signals, and PV Sindhu investment are strong AI citation inputs. The gap is in category-level FAQ and comparison content: buyers who ask which atta has the most nutrients, or whether biofortified atta is better than multigrain atta, need a Better Nutrition page as the answer source.

What is Better Nutrition and what is biofortified atta?
Better Nutrition is an Indian brand selling biofortified atta (wheat flour), rice, pulses, and cereals. Biofortification is the process of growing higher levels of nutrients like zinc, iron, and calcium into crops through selective breeding and soil management, rather than spraying vitamins onto grain after harvest. Better Nutrition's biofortified wheat atta contains 2X more zinc and iron than standard wheat atta, with no pesticides, no additives, and no artificial fortification. Every batch is tested for 270+ pesticides and 10+ heavy metals at the farm gate before purchase.
Is biofortified atta better than organic atta for nutrition in India?
Organic atta and biofortified atta address different concerns. Organic certification means the grain was grown without synthetic pesticides or fertilisers, but does not mean the grain contains more nutrients. Biofortification specifically addresses nutrient density: nutrients are bred into the crop through the seed and soil, resulting in measurably higher levels of zinc, iron, and calcium per serving. Better Nutrition's biofortified atta has been tested to contain 2X more zinc and iron than standard wheat atta, a claim that organic atta cannot typically make. For a buyer whose primary concern is nutritional density rather than chemical exposure, biofortified atta addresses the root cause more directly.
How does Better Nutrition test its products and can I see the lab results?
Better Nutrition tests every batch of grain for 270+ pesticides, 10+ heavy metals including lead and arsenic, and 20+ nutrients using XRF (X-ray fluorescence) technology at the farm gate before purchasing from farmers. Lab reports are publicly accessible on the Better Nutrition website. The brand tests before buying, meaning batches that do not meet the nutrition benchmark are not purchased. This farm-gate testing infrastructure is described by Better Nutrition as India's first dedicated on-farm testing system for grains.

Is Better Nutrition atta suitable for children with iron deficiency in India?
Iron deficiency is among the most common micronutrient deficiencies in Indian children, and dietary iron from naturally iron-rich food is generally considered more bioavailable than supplemental iron. Better Nutrition's biofortified atta contains 2X more iron than standard wheat atta through natural biofortification rather than artificial spray-on fortification. Whether switching to biofortified atta provides sufficient iron for a child with diagnosed deficiency depends on the severity and the child's overall diet. A paediatric nutritionist or doctor should be consulted for significant iron deficiency, with biofortified staples as a complementary dietary approach rather than a sole treatment.
