What AI engines see today

Artociti is an Indian manufacturer and exporter of wall art, traditional paintings, and home decor items, available at artociti.com. The brand covers traditional Indian art, modern paintings, and decorative pieces delivered to homes across India. In tested queries for wall art for living room India, best Indian traditional art prints online, and art gifts for home India, Artociti appears infrequently. Most AI-generated responses in these query categories cite marketplace listings or brands with richer educational content rather than Artociti’s owned website. The brand’s manufacturer positioning and product range represent an untapped AI citation foundation.

Why this score matters
Artociti operates in a category where buyers research extensively before purchasing. Wall art and home decor purchases involve questions about art style, material quality, sizing, framing, and placement. These are exactly the buyer questions AI engines answer using structured educational content. A brand that manufactures wall art has direct expertise to answer these questions authoritatively, yet Artociti’s website is structured as a product catalog rather than an answer resource. Buyers who ask ChatGPT what is the best type of wall art for an Indian home or how to choose art prints for a living room do not reach Artociti in the AI response. The brand is invisible to the research phase of a purchase decision it should be winning.

Content Gaps Costing AI Citations

3 gaps found
Traditional Indian art styles for home decor: ChatGPT answer without Artociti
Traditional Indian art is a growing home decor category driven by buyers who want culturally rooted interiors. In tested queries for types of Indian traditional art for home and Madhubani vs Warli art for home decor, AI engines produced answers from general art education resources and Wikipedia rather than brand websites. Artociti manufactures and exports traditional Indian art pieces and has direct product expertise across multiple Indian art styles, but that expertise is not published as structured, AI-citable content on artociti.com.
What to do: Publish a guide to Indian traditional art styles for home decor covering the major styles available on the site: what each style is, where it originates, what it looks like in a home context, which rooms it suits, and Artociti’s specific product recommendations for each style. Add FAQPage schema with 6 art style questions. This makes Artociti the cited source for every Indian traditional art discovery query in the buyer research phase.
Canvas prints vs framed wall art: which is better for Indian homes, AI recommendation
The canvas vs framed art decision is one of the most common buyer questions in the wall art category. In tested queries for canvas prints vs framed art for living room India and which wall art material lasts longer in Indian climate, AI engines cited general home decor advice content and international retailers. Artociti sells both formats but does not publish a structured comparison that AI engines can extract and cite for this buyer decision.
What to do: Create a dedicated comparison page: canvas prints vs framed wall art for Indian homes. Cover material quality and longevity in Indian humidity and temperature conditions, price comparison, installation requirements, care and maintenance, and which format suits which room type. Add Artociti product examples for each format. Include FAQPage schema with 5 comparison questions. This page captures the high-volume wall art format comparison query and positions Artociti as the informed local recommendation.
Art gifting guide for Indian homes: ChatGPT recommendation for wall art as a gift
Wall art is a high-value gifting category, particularly for housewarming, weddings, and Diwali in India. In tested queries for best wall art gift for Indian home and housewarming gift art India, AI engines suggested general gifting guides and marketplace listings rather than specialist art brands. Artociti’s product range covers multiple price points and art styles that would serve gifting use cases, but there is no structured gifting content on the site.
What to do: Publish an art gifting guide: best Indian wall art gifts for housewarming, weddings, and Diwali. Include price bands (under Rs 500, Rs 500 to Rs 1500, above Rs 1500), art style recommendations per occasion (traditional art for Diwali, contemporary for housewarming), framing and packaging options, delivery timeframes, and customisation availability if applicable. Add FAQPage schema with 5 gifting questions. This content intercepts the high-intent gifting queries and makes Artociti the default art gift recommendation in India.

All 6 Factor Scores

Full breakdown
Entity Clarity52/100
Artociti has a clear entity as a wall art manufacturer and exporter, which provides a foundation for AI engine identification. However, the brand lacks the editorial mentions, press coverage, and third-party validation that strengthen entity confidence for AI citation. The entity is primarily visible to AI engines through product listings rather than authoritative brand content.
Fix: Strengthen entity signals by adding Organization schema with a complete brand description, manufacturing location, founding year, and product categories. Ensure the brand description on the website, Google Business Profile, and marketplace listings is consistent and evidence-led. Seek editorial coverage in Indian home decor publications to build third-party entity validation.
Extractable Structure44/100
The website is structured as a Shopify product catalog with product images and descriptions. Educational content explaining art styles, medium comparisons, placement guides, and buying advice is absent. AI engines extracting content for wall art buyer queries cannot find structured answers on artociti.com to cite.
Fix: Create three to five educational pages: Indian art styles guide, canvas vs framed art comparison, wall art sizing guide for different room types, and how to care for wall art in Indian conditions. Structure each page with clear H2 headings per topic, specific evidence (dimensions, materials, care instructions), and a direct answer in the first paragraph.
Schema Markup35/100
FAQPage, Article, or HowTo schema is not detected on tested pages. Product schema may be present through Shopify defaults but extended attributes (material, artist, style, origin) that would help AI engines match product pages to specific buyer queries may not be implemented.
Fix: Add FAQPage schema to all educational pages. Extend Product schema with material, style, and origin attributes for each art category. Add Organization schema to the homepage with the brand’s full entity description. These three changes create a schema-backed content layer that AI engines can extract and cite for buyer queries.
FAQ Coverage32/100
FAQ content is effectively absent on the tested pages. The most commonly asked questions about wall art in India (art styles, sizing, material quality, care, gifting, placement) are not answered on the site. This is the largest single gap in Artociti’s AI visibility and the most straightforward to address.
Fix: Publish buyer question pages for the top 5 query types in the wall art category: which art style for which room, how to size wall art for an Indian living room, which art material lasts longest in Indian conditions, how to choose a gift art piece, and traditional vs modern art for Indian homes. Each page should open with a direct answer and include FAQPage schema.
Answer-Led Content40/100
Product descriptions on artociti.com tend to lead with product names and style descriptions rather than direct answers to buyer questions. A page that opens with what this art piece is, who it is for, and what makes it distinctive is more likely to be cited by AI engines than a page that opens with a product name.
Fix: Revise the opening copy of each product category page to lead with a direct answer to the implied buyer question. For example, a traditional Madhubani art category page should open with: These are hand-painted Madhubani art prints from Bihar, India. They suit living rooms and pooja rooms in traditional and contemporary Indian homes and are available as canvas prints and framed pieces from Rs 499.
Specificity & Evidence48/100
Some product descriptions include material and size information. However, specific production details (handmade vs printed, artist region, material specifications), care information, and evidence of quality (certifications, customer count, years of experience) are inconsistently present across the catalog.
Fix: Add specific production evidence to all product pages: whether the piece is handmade or printed, the art region of origin, the specific material (canvas type, frame material, print process), dimensions in centimetres, and any quality certifications. Include the brand’s manufacturing experience and customer count as entity credibility signals on the homepage and About page.
