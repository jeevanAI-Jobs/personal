Executive Summary

Key Findings

Most brands are significantly less visible in AI-generated responses than they are in traditional search. In tested queries across B2B SaaS, fintech, healthtech, and e-commerce categories, the median brand appeared in fewer than one in four relevant AI responses. The top quartile of brands appeared in more than half of relevant queries and were actively recommended in more than a third.

The gap between top and median performers suggests that AI visibility is not yet evenly distributed and that brands acting early on the foundational signals may gain durable advantages before their categories become more competitive in AI responses.

Finding 01

Entity clarity is the strongest predictor

Brands with well-defined entities (Organization schema, Wikidata presence, consistent third-party descriptions) appear more frequently and more accurately across all tested platforms.

Finding 02

Review platforms drive recommendation rate

In tested recommendation queries ("which tool should I use for X"), brands with active review platform listings appeared significantly more often than those without, regardless of content volume.

Finding 03

Platform coverage is uneven for most brands

Most tested brands had their strongest AI visibility on one platform and notably weaker visibility on others, suggesting that platform-specific signals matter alongside shared foundational ones.

Finding 04

Content structure matters more than volume

Brands with well-structured, directly-answering content were cited more often than brands with higher content volume but less structured pages, across both RAG-based and training-data-based platforms.

Finding 05

New brands face a measurable cold start disadvantage

Brands less than two years old showed consistently lower AI visibility scores at baseline. The fastest-recovering new brands addressed entity signals and review platform presence within the first 90 days.

Finding 06

AI descriptions are often inaccurate

In testing, approximately one in three brands had at least one significant inaccuracy in how AI systems described them, most commonly incorrect category positioning or outdated product information.

Understanding Your AI Visibility Score

The AI Visibility Score is a composite metric ranging from 0 to 100, combining mention rate, recommendation rate, and citation rate across tested platforms, weighted by query type and platform reach. It is designed to give teams a single trackable number while preserving the ability to decompose it into components for diagnosis.

Score range What it suggests Typical profile
0–15 Very low AI visibility New brand or brand with minimal entity signals, no review platform presence. AI systems rarely mention the brand in category queries.
16–30 Below-median visibility Brand is known to AI systems but appears inconsistently. May have entity signals but limited corroboration or structured content.
31–50 Median visibility Appears regularly in AI responses for category queries. Has foundational signals in place. May have gaps in recommendation rate or citation rate.
51–70 Above-median visibility Strong entity definition, review platform presence, and content structure. Appears in most relevant category queries and is actively recommended.
71–100 Top-quartile visibility All foundational signals in place. Strong corroboration across multiple independent sources. Consistent, accurate AI descriptions. High recommendation rate.

How to Improve Your Score

Based on observed patterns, the following actions appear most likely to improve AI visibility scores, roughly in order of impact-to-effort ratio:

  1. Define your brand entity clearly. Implement Organization schema on your homepage. Create or update your Wikidata entry. Write a single, accurate one-sentence brand description and use it verbatim across all third-party sources. See the brand entity definition for specifics.
  2. Build review platform presence. Create listings on the one or two review platforms most used in your category (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, or category-specific alternatives). Encourage existing customers to leave reviews. See brand corroboration for the full list of corroborating sources.
  3. Structure content for AI citability. Ensure your most important pages answer specific questions directly in the first one to two sentences of each section. Add FAQPage schema to key pages. See the GEO content brief definition for the full brief structure.
  4. Earn editorial coverage. Press mentions in respected industry publications carry significant corroborating weight. A sustained PR effort that generates three to five editorial mentions per quarter is a meaningful input into AI visibility over time.
  5. Monitor and address inaccuracies. Check what AI systems say about your brand regularly. Inaccurate descriptions should be corrected at the source (schema, Wikidata, your own content) before they compound. See brand information accuracy.

Trends to Watch in 2026–2027

Based on observed platform changes and the direction of AI system development, the following trends appear likely to affect brand AI visibility over the next twelve months:

  • More frequent retrieval updates. RAG-based platforms are moving toward more frequent retrieval index updates, which may reduce the lag between a brand's content changes and their effect on AI visibility.
  • Greater platform differentiation. As AI platforms develop distinct user bases and use patterns, the visibility profile of a brand across platforms may diverge further, making multi-platform tracking more important.
  • Recommendation selectivity increasing. Early patterns in tested queries suggest that the number of brands recommended per query may decrease over time as AI systems become more confident in their recommendations, making top-quartile positioning more valuable and harder to displace.
  • Entity disambiguation becoming critical. As more brands enter AI-researched categories, the risk of entity confusion increases. Brands with clearly differentiated entity definitions may maintain more accurate AI descriptions than those with generic positioning language.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good AI Visibility Score?

Based on tested queries across B2B SaaS categories, brands in the top quartile tend to show AI Visibility Scores above 60 out of 100. The median across tested brands falls between 25 and 40. New brands with limited entity signals often score below 15 initially. These ranges vary significantly by category and competitive density.

Which AI platform gives brands the most visibility?

In tested queries, Perplexity tends to cite more brands per response than ChatGPT, which tends to be more selective in its recommendations. Google AI Overviews shows high variability depending on whether a query triggers an AI Overview at all. The platform mix matters: a brand may have strong visibility on Perplexity and low visibility on ChatGPT, requiring different optimization approaches for each.

How long does it take to improve AI visibility?

Based on observed patterns, brands that address foundational gaps (entity definition, Wikidata presence, review platform listings) may see changes in RAG-based platforms like Perplexity within four to eight weeks. Changes in training-data-based systems like ChatGPT tend to appear over longer cycles. Brands starting from near-zero visibility typically require three to six months of consistent effort to reach median category benchmarks.

What is the biggest factor in AI visibility?

Across tested brands, entity clarity appears to be the single most important factor. Brands that are clearly defined in structured sources (Organization schema, Wikidata, review platforms using consistent language) tend to appear more frequently and more accurately in AI responses than brands with equivalent content but weaker entity signals. Review platform presence is the second most impactful factor for recommendation-type queries.
