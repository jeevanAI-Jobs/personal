90-Day GEO Roadmap: From Zero AI Visibility to Measurable Citations

A week-by-week plan to go from invisible in AI search to consistently cited. Built for B2B SaaS brands starting their GEO program from scratch.

Sep 7, 2026  •  10 min read  •  Jeevan AI Research

A 2026 study by Forrester found that 68% of B2B marketers identify AI search visibility as a top priority, yet only 14% have a documented GEO strategy with measurable milestones. The gap between recognizing the importance and knowing where to start is where most programs stall. This roadmap closes that gap. It is a 90-day plan structured across three phases, with specific deliverables, owners, and checkpoints at each stage.

Ninety days is the right planning horizon because the first signals from GEO work become measurable around day 60 to 70, giving you enough data to course-correct before the quarter ends. It is not a magic number; some categories see faster movement, others slower. But 90 days is long enough to do real work and short enough to maintain team momentum.

Before Day 1: Setting the Baseline

Before any execution begins, you need a baseline. Without one, you are running blind. A GEO baseline takes roughly two to three hours to establish and should be documented in a shared spreadsheet.

Your baseline should capture, for a set of 15 to 20 category-level queries run across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini: which brands are mentioned, how many times your brand is cited, how accurately your brand is described, and which competitors appear most frequently. Run the same queries again at day 30, day 60, and day 90. This is your scoreboard.

The baseline queries should represent how your target buyer would actually research your category in AI search. Include problem-framing queries ("how do I solve X"), comparison queries ("X vs Y for Z use case"), and evaluation queries ("best tools for X"). If you have not yet run a structured baseline, the GEO audit checklist covers the full baseline setup process including which query types to prioritize.

Phase 1: Foundation (Days 1 to 30)

Phase 1 Goal: Build the structural layer that AI engines read

By the end of day 30, every critical page should have schema markup, your entity listings should be complete and consistent, and your Bing presence should be verified.

Week 1: Technical Foundation

  • Add Organization schema to your homepage
  • Add SoftwareApplication or Product schema to every product page
  • Add FAQPage schema to your top 5 to 10 blog posts and landing pages
  • Submit your sitemap to Bing Webmaster Tools and verify indexing
  • Update your robots.txt to allow AI crawler access (GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot)
  • Check and fix any llms.txt file if present

Week 2: Entity Listings

  • Audit your Crunchbase, G2, LinkedIn, and Product Hunt profiles for consistency
  • Ensure your company description uses the same category language across every listing
  • Update your Wikipedia article if one exists (or assess notability for a new one)
  • Respond to any unanswered G2 reviews from the past 90 days
  • Verify your Wikidata entity if applicable

Weeks 3 to 4: Product Page Rewrite

  • Rewrite your main product page to include natural language answers to the top 5 evaluation questions your buyers ask AI
  • Add a clear "Who is this for" section with audience and use-case language
  • Add an "Alternatives" or "How we compare" section
  • Include specific outcome data: customer results, time-to-value, integration compatibility

Phase 2: Content Build (Days 31 to 60)

Phase 2 Goal: Create the content AI retrieves when buyers ask evaluation questions

By the end of day 60, you should have 6 to 8 new AI-optimized content assets and the first external citation placements secured.

Weeks 5 to 6: Core Comparison Content

Comparison content is the highest-priority content format for GEO. Write one comprehensive comparison page for each of your top 3 competitors. Each page should include a fair assessment of strengths and weaknesses, a clear use-case fit table, migration considerations, and pricing context. These pages should link to each other and to your product page using descriptive internal anchor text.

When writing these pages, follow the structural content patterns outlined in this GEO content brief template to ensure each asset is structured for AI retrieval, not just for human readers.

Weeks 7 to 8: Category Guides and Use Case Pages

  • Write one comprehensive "Best [category] tools for [audience type]" guide
  • Write two use-case specific pages targeting the queries your GSC data shows you are appearing for but not converting
  • Draft one FAQ page specifically answering the 15 most common evaluation questions for your category

Day 60 Checkpoint

Re-run your baseline query set and compare. At this stage, look specifically for: Perplexity and Copilot beginning to cite your new pages, any shift in how accurately AI engines describe your product, and whether competitor citation rates have changed. Document what is working and cut what is not before entering Phase 3.

Phase 3: Authority and Distribution (Days 61 to 90)

Phase 3 Goal: Earn external citations and grow AI share of voice

By day 90, your brand should appear in AI responses for at least 30% of your target query set, with accurate descriptions and positive framing.

Weeks 9 to 10: Earned Coverage

  • Identify 5 industry publications or newsletters that your target buyer reads
  • Pitch a data-driven article, guest post, or expert quote opportunity to each
  • Submit your product for review to one relevant analyst or research firm
  • Seed 3 to 5 high-quality community responses on Reddit or specialized forums where your category is discussed

Weeks 11 to 12: Measurement and Iteration

Run your full query set a final time. Produce a one-page summary for your team showing: baseline citation rate vs. current citation rate, which query types you have won, which competitors you have displaced, and which gaps remain. This summary becomes the input for your Q4 GEO plan.

Track not just citation frequency but citation quality. An AI that mentions your brand dismissively or incorrectly is worse than no mention at all. Understanding how to measure AI share of voice across dimensions is covered in the complete guide to measuring AI share of voice for B2B brands.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to see results from GEO?

Most brands see measurable improvement in AI citation rates within 60 to 90 days of consistent GEO work. The first visible signal is usually Perplexity or Copilot beginning to cite your content in web-grounded queries, since these engines refresh their retrieval indexes more frequently than ChatGPT's training cycle.

What is the most important first step in a GEO program?

The most important first step is establishing a baseline: running a consistent set of 15 to 20 queries across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini to record your current citation rate and competitor citation rates. Without a baseline, you cannot measure improvement and you risk optimizing in the wrong direction.

How much of GEO is content versus technical?

For most B2B SaaS brands, content is approximately 70% of the GEO effort and technical implementation is about 30%. The technical work (schema markup, entity listings, Bing indexing, robots.txt) provides the foundation. The content work (comparison pages, use-case guides, community seeding) drives the actual citation rate.

Do I need a dedicated team for GEO?

Not necessarily. A successful GEO program can be run by a content marketer with support from a developer for technical implementation. The key is consistency: one person who owns the quarterly query tracking and monthly content cadence will outperform a large team that treats GEO as a one-time project.
