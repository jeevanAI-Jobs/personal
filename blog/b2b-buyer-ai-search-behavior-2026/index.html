How B2B Buyers Use AI Search to Research Vendors in 2026

Your buyer opens ChatGPT before they open your website. Here is exactly what they ask, what they see, and why your brand may not be on their shortlist.

The short version: B2B buyers now start vendor research on ChatGPT or Perplexity, not Google. They ask four types of questions across the buying journey. If your brand does not appear in the AI answers to those questions, you are being filtered out before the buyer ever reaches your website. This guide covers the full buyer journey, the specific queries buyers use, and what brands need to do to appear in those answers.

A sales manager at a software company recently shared something that stopped me. Their team had noticed a pattern: prospects who scheduled demos consistently mentioned they had already "researched options on ChatGPT." When they dug into the data, roughly 35 percent of qualified demo requests that quarter had zero referrer data. Dark traffic. Buyers who arrived knowing exactly what they wanted and why.

This is not an edge case anymore. It is the default buying behavior for a growing share of B2B buyers, especially in tech, professional services, and anything where the buyer is a digitally fluent professional. The buying journey has fundamentally changed, and most brands are optimizing for a version of it that no longer exists.

The New B2B Buying Journey

The traditional B2B buying journey started with a Google search, a scroll through 10 blue links, a few website visits, and then maybe a comparison on G2. That journey still exists, but a parallel one has emerged that starts earlier, moves faster, and produces a shorter shortlist.

The AI-assisted buying journey has four distinct stages, each with a different type of query:

1
Problem Framing
The buyer is trying to understand their problem before they know what kind of solution to look for. They ask AI to explain the problem, its causes, and what good looks like.
Example AI query
"Why do SaaS companies struggle to get recommended by ChatGPT to buyers?"
2
Category Discovery
The buyer asks AI what type of solution exists for their problem. This is the stage where vendor shortlists are built. Brands that appear here have an enormous advantage.
Example AI query
"What tools do B2B SaaS companies use to monitor and improve their AI search visibility?"
3
Comparison and Shortlisting
The buyer compares 2-3 shortlisted vendors directly. They ask AI to explain differences, pricing, strengths, and weaknesses. This stage is where positioning matters most.
Example AI query
"Compare Jeevan AI vs [competitor] for a 50-person B2B SaaS team tracking AI citations"
4
Validation and Risk Reduction
Before reaching out, the buyer validates their choice. They ask AI about customer satisfaction, reliability, and whether the vendor is trustworthy for their use case.
Example AI query
"Is Jeevan AI used by funded SaaS startups? What do customers say about it?"

Notice what is absent from all four stages: a visit to your website. By the time the buyer clicks "Schedule a Demo," they may already know your pricing tier, your core use case, your primary competitors, and at least a summary of customer sentiment. All from AI responses they read before your website ever loaded.

How AI Search Compresses the Funnel

Traditional B2B buying journeys lasted weeks or months across dozens of touchpoints. AI search compresses this in two ways.

First, buyers get synthesized answers instead of a list of links. A Google search for "best AI visibility tools" returns 10 links plus ads. A ChatGPT query for the same returns a narrative answer that names 3-5 vendors, explains what each does well, and often makes a recommendation. The buyer skips the comparison phase that used to happen on G2 or through analyst reports.

Second, AI responses carry implicit authority. When ChatGPT says "for B2B SaaS teams, [Vendor X] is the most commonly recommended tool," a buyer reads that as a peer-consensus recommendation even though they have no idea how that answer was generated. This implicit authority means brands that appear in AI answers carry more weight than brands the buyer finds through a random Google click.

The dark traffic signal

If more than 25 percent of your demo requests or trial signups arrive with no referrer data, a significant portion of those are likely AI-assisted buyers. They researched you in a private ChatGPT session, then navigated directly to your site. This is now a standard pattern in B2B tech buying, not an anomaly.

What AI Tells Buyers About Your Category

The most important query type is Stage 2: category discovery. This is where buyers ask "what tools do companies use for X" and AI builds a shortlist. The brands that appear consistently in these answers win a disproportionate share of buyer attention.

How AI builds category shortlists is not random. It draws on several signals:

Brands that rank well on Google but score poorly on these five signals are often invisible in AI category answers. This is the core of the visibility gap most B2B companies are now facing.

What Brands Need to Change

Most B2B brands are still optimizing for the 2022 buyer journey. They invest in SEO, content marketing for Google rankings, and ad campaigns that drive website traffic. These are not wrong, but they are incomplete for a buyer who now starts on ChatGPT.

The brands that are winning AI-assisted buyers are doing three things differently:

1. They publish content for AI query patterns, not search keywords

AI buyers ask conversational, problem-framing questions. They do not type "AI visibility tool pricing." They ask "how much should a 30-person SaaS team budget for AI visibility monitoring?" Content written to answer the second type of question appears in AI responses. Content optimized for the first type mostly appears in Google's blue links. Both are valuable, but they require different content structures.

2. They build entity authority across third-party sources

AI systems are pattern-matchers. They recommend brands they have seen described consistently, in the same category terms, across many independent sources. Building this consistency means filling in G2, Capterra, and TrustRadius profiles completely, publishing press releases that use consistent brand and category language, and earning editorial placements that describe the brand the same way the brand describes itself.

3. They measure AI share of voice alongside traditional metrics

Brands that track their AI share of voice can see whether their efforts are working before it shows up in revenue. Without this measurement, AI visibility is a black box. Teams optimize for Google rankings and hope AI follows. It often does not, because the signals are different.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are B2B buyers actually using ChatGPT to research vendors?

Yes. Research from 2025 and 2026 consistently shows 65-80 percent of B2B buyers use AI tools during vendor research. The most common use case is early-stage category exploration before visiting any vendor website.

What queries do B2B buyers ask AI when researching vendors?

Four types: problem-framing ("why do companies struggle with X"), category discovery ("what tools exist for Y"), comparison ("vendor A vs vendor B"), and validation ("is vendor C trusted for enterprise"). Category discovery queries are the most critical because that is where shortlists form.

How is AI-assisted buying different from traditional Google research?

AI synthesizes an answer rather than showing links, produces a 3-5 vendor shortlist rather than 10 links, and draws from different sources (review platforms, editorial roundups, structured content) than Google's ranking factors.

How do I know if AI search is affecting my pipeline?

Look for dark traffic (no referrer) on demo requests and signups. Add "how did you hear about us" to forms with AI tools as an option. In my experience, brands find 20-40 percent of self-reported discovery comes from AI tools within months of enabling this question.
