Watching Competitors' Own Ads Instead of Guessing at Their Strategy

Share of voice tells you how often you're named compared to competitors in AI answers. Pulling their actual live Google and Meta ads tells you what they're spending on right now. Together they turn competitive guesswork into something you can track.

Jeevan AI calculates share of voice, your brand's presence in AI answers measured against named competitors on the same questions, and separately pulls each competitor's top active Google and Meta ads so a team can see not just who is winning the AI answer, but what that competitor is actively messaging and spending on. Meta's ad data is known to be less complete than Google's, and that gap matters for how confidently a team should read a result.

Most competitive research starts with a guess. Someone on the team has a hunch about what a competitor is emphasizing this quarter, based on a sales call that went sideways or a landing page someone happened to see. That hunch might be right. It also might be eight months stale, or based on a campaign the competitor already killed.

Two different signals replace the guess with something closer to evidence: how often a competitor is actually named in AI answers compared to you, and what that competitor is actually running as live advertising right now. Neither one alone is the full picture. Together, they are close to as good a read on a competitor's real current strategy as a small team can get without paying for enterprise competitive intelligence tooling.

This is the use case behind Jeevan AI's share of voice calculation and competitor ad intelligence pull, covered alongside the broader context in how different AI visibility tools approach competitive tracking.

What share of voice actually measures

Share of voice takes the same set of tested buyer questions a brand's visibility scan runs, and instead of scoring the brand in isolation, it measures how often the brand is named relative to how often each tracked competitor is named, across that same question set.

The reframing matters because a brand can look fine on an absolute score and still be quietly losing ground. A brand named in forty percent of relevant answers feels respectable, until it becomes clear a single competitor is named in seventy percent of the same answers. Share of voice surfaces that comparison directly instead of leaving a team to infer it from two separate reports.

It also updates every time the scan runs, so it is not a one-time snapshot of who is winning, it is a trend a team can watch move as both sides' content and citations change.

How Jeevan AI makes competitor tracking easy without manual digging

Finding out what a competitor is currently running as ads has traditionally meant manually checking Google's and Meta's own ad transparency tools, competitor by competitor, with no history and no way to compare across a tracked set.

Top active ads, pulled automatically

For each tracked competitor, Jeevan AI pulls their current top active Google and Meta ads directly, so a team sees actual running creative and messaging rather than a stale screenshot someone saved three months ago. The cost of each pull is kept intentionally low, around seventy-five paise, so checking in on a competitor is not a decision a team has to ration.

Share of voice next to the ad data, not in a separate tool

Because share of voice and ad intelligence live in the same dashboard, a team can connect the two: a competitor whose share of voice is climbing and whose ad spend shows a new campaign theme is a different, more urgent situation than a competitor whose share of voice is flat and whose ads haven't changed in months.

Correctly attributed to the right advertiser

Ad intelligence pulls are matched to the correct advertiser account rather than a loosely similar brand name or a reseller, which matters more than it sounds for competitors with common names or multiple regional entities running separate ad accounts.

Why checking once tells you almost nothing about a moving target

A competitor's ad strategy and AI answer presence are not static. Ad campaigns start and stop on timelines a single check will almost always miss. Share of voice shifts as content gets published, citations age, and AI platforms update what they draw on.

A single pull answers "what does this look like today." It says nothing about direction. Checking on a regular rhythm, ideally alongside the same cadence as a brand's own visibility re-scans, is what turns a snapshot into a trend: is a competitor's share of voice climbing or shrinking, is their ad spend ramping into a specific theme or winding down.

Treating competitor monitoring as a recurring habit rather than a one-time competitive audit is the same discipline this applies to everywhere else in AI visibility work, a single data point is noisy, a trend across several checks is a signal.

Why this beats manually checking ad transparency tools yourself

Nothing stops a team from manually opening Google's and Meta's ad transparency pages for each competitor on their own. What that approach lacks is the connection back to share of voice, the low-friction cost that makes frequent checking realistic, and the correct advertiser matching that keeps the data from being quietly wrong.

It is worth being honest about the limits here too. Meta's ad library interface has known gaps, and a result showing no active ads for a competitor on Meta should be read as inconclusive rather than as proof they are running nothing. Google's data tends to be more complete. Reading both with that distinction in mind is part of using this responsibly, not a reason to distrust the signal entirely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is share of voice in AI search visibility?

Share of voice is the proportion of tested buyer questions where your brand is named in the AI answer, measured against how often named competitors show up on the same set of questions. It reframes visibility as relative standing in a category rather than a standalone score, since a brand that looks fine in isolation can still be losing ground to a competitor who is named far more often on the same questions.

Why look at competitors' actual running ads instead of just their AI answer mentions?

AI answer mentions show what AI tools are currently saying, which is downstream of a lot of marketing effort that already happened. A competitor's live Google and Meta ads show what they are actively spending on and messaging around right now, closer to a leading indicator of where their strategy is headed than their AI citation footprint is.

Is ad intelligence data equally reliable across Google and Meta?

No. Google's ad transparency data tends to be more complete and consistent to pull. Meta's ad library interface has known gaps and is less reliable for some advertisers, so a true absence of ads in a pull should be treated as inconclusive on Meta rather than confirmed, while a Google result is generally more trustworthy as a direct read.

Guessing at a competitor's strategy from a stale impression is the default most teams fall back on simply because real competitive data has historically been slow and manual to assemble. Share of voice and competitor ad intelligence replace that guess with two connected, regularly refreshed signals: how you compare in AI answers, and what your competitors are actually running right now.

Reading both with their real limits in mind, Meta's gaps especially, and checking on a recurring rhythm rather than once, is what turns this from an interesting one-time report into an actual input to strategy.
