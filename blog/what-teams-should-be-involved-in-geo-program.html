What Teams Should Be Involved in a GEO Programme

Jeevan AI · 2026-10-16 · 8 min read
Key Points
  • AI visibility work crosses marketing, content, SEO, and product functions simultaneously
  • In most organisations, no single team owns GEO today, which is the primary reason progress stalls
  • A practical RACI model for assigning GEO responsibilities without adding headcount
  • The three team configurations that work at different company sizes

Why GEO Falls Between Teams

Generative Engine Optimisation sits at the intersection of content strategy, technical SEO, product marketing, and brand communications. It requires content decisions, data tracking, schema implementation, and regular cross-functional review cycles. In most B2B organisations, each of those functions belongs to a different team, which is precisely why GEO programmes stall after initial enthusiasm.

The symptom is familiar: an audit happens, gaps are identified, a content plan is drafted, and then nothing moves because it is unclear who approves the content brief, who writes the post, who tracks whether it worked, and who escalates when a competitor gains ground.

GEO is not an SEO task, a content task, or a brand task. It is a cross-functional programme that needs explicit ownership to produce results.

Three Team Configurations That Work

Configuration 1: SEO-Led (works for 1 to 50 person companies)
The SEO manager or content lead owns the GEO programme. They run scans, produce or brief content, implement schema, and report monthly. Works well when the SEO function has direct content production capacity and stakeholder support for a quarterly review cadence.

Configuration 2: Growth-Led (works for 50 to 200 person companies)
A growth or demand generation lead owns AI visibility as a pipeline input metric. Content, SEO, and product marketing are collaborating functions with defined inputs. The growth team owns the tracking dashboard and escalation process. Works well when GEO is framed as a pipeline metric rather than a marketing content metric.

Configuration 3: Dedicated GEO Role (works for 200+ person companies or agencies)
A dedicated AI visibility specialist or GEO manager owns the programme end-to-end. They commission content from the content team, work with SEO on technical implementation, brief product marketing on positioning gaps, and present quarterly results to the CMO. This model has the highest programme quality but requires budget for a role or external resource.

The Most Common Mistake

The most common mistake is assigning GEO to the content team without giving them scan data, competitor benchmarks, or a prioritised query list. Content teams produce great writing. They do not automatically know which buying queries to target, what competitors are winning on, or whether a post they published three months ago moved the needle on AI recommendations.

The solution is separating the data function from the content function. Someone needs to own the scan cycle, interpret the results, and translate them into a prioritised brief. That person does not need to be a data scientist. They need access to a tracking tool and a standing meeting with the content team every four to six weeks.

Getting Started Without a Dedicated Role

Most teams do not have budget for a dedicated GEO hire in 2026. The practical starting point is: assign one named person to own the scan cycle and the monthly report, even if they spend only a few hours on it per month. The scan tool does the data collection. The named person does the interpretation and escalation.

That person does not need GEO expertise on day one. The scan data tells them where the gaps are. The content team fills the gaps. The next scan shows whether the fills are working. The feedback loop is self-teaching if someone owns it.

Frequently Asked Questions

In most B2B SaaS companies, GEO works best when a growth or demand generation lead owns the programme with defined contributions from content, SEO, and product marketing. The growth lead owns tracking and reporting; content produces AI-optimised posts; SEO handles schema and technical implementation; product marketing ensures AI describes the product accurately.
A minimal viable GEO team structure requires one person to own the scan cycle and monthly reporting, one content resource to produce AI-optimised content based on scan data, and periodic input from an SEO resource for schema and technical work. Full-time dedicated headcount is not required until the programme scales to multiple brands or more than 100 tracked queries.
In most organisations, AI visibility results should be accountable to the CMO or Head of Growth, with operational ownership sitting with the person who runs the scan cycle. Without C-level accountability, GEO programmes tend to stall when content gaps require cross-functional resource allocation.
A lean GEO programme with 25 to 50 tracked queries, bi-monthly scans, and monthly reporting requires roughly 4 to 8 hours per month from the programme owner, plus content production time. With a tracking tool handling the scan cycle and reporting automatically, the programme owner's time drops to 2 to 3 hours per month for interpretation and briefing.
