ChatGPT Business Hub: The Complete Guide to Businesses in ChatGPT

Who can claim, every verification step, what each profile field does, the product feed spec field by field, Business Agents, sponsored agents, the limits OpenAI is open about, and what all of it means if your brand is in India.

Part of the ChatGPT Visibility Hub: every Jeevan AI guide to getting your brand named and recommended in ChatGPT, in one place.

Short version. On November 10, 2026, OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. Through a dashboard called Business Hub, a business can claim its profile, keep details current, upload a product feed and see aggregated insights (mentions, website clicks, discovery topics). Eligible U.S. ecommerce and online service businesses can also publish a Business Agent. Claiming is free and worth doing, but OpenAI is explicit that it does not guarantee more visibility or control ChatGPT's independent recommendations. This guide is built only from OpenAI's announcement, help center, policies and developer docs, and every limit is marked.

For two years, the only way to see how ChatGPT described your brand was from the outside: type questions, screenshot answers, guess at the sources. Businesses in ChatGPT is the first time OpenAI has given companies a formal way in. You can now supply your own facts, see first-party numbers, and in some markets let customers talk to an agent that represents you.

It is also easy to misread. A claimed profile is not a ranking boost, a verified badge is not a recommendation, and a mention in Business Hub is not the same thing as your brand being named in an answer. Teams that treat the launch as "claim it and we're done" may be disappointed. Teams that treat it as one new input, set up carefully and measured honestly, are likely to get real value from it.

This guide walks through every part of the program in the order you will meet it, with the rules that OpenAI itself has published.

What OpenAI Launched on November 10, 2026

OpenAI introduced Businesses in ChatGPT as a beta, framing it around the 1.2 billion people it says use ChatGPT each week. The program has four parts that people often blur together:

One more name causes confusion: ChatGPT Business is OpenAI's paid workspace plan for teams. It has nothing to do with Business Hub.

In one sentence: Businesses in ChatGPT lets a verified business supply its own facts and product data to ChatGPT, see aggregated engagement numbers, and in the U.S. offer a labeled agent, while ChatGPT's own recommendations stay independent of both the profile and any ad spend.

What Business Hub Can and Cannot Change

Before any setup, it helps to be clear about the boundary OpenAI has drawn. Its help center says claiming "doesn't guarantee increased visibility" and does not give you direct control over ChatGPT's independent recommendations. It also says ChatGPT may use other sources when answering questions about your business, and that advertising does not influence ChatGPT's independent answers.

So the profile is best understood as a source of truth you now own, not a lever on rankings. The work that tends to change recommendations still happens outside the dashboard, which we cover in the last section and across the ChatGPT Visibility Hub.

Who Can Claim a Business in ChatGPT

OpenAI's rules, as published in the help center and the Businesses in ChatGPT policy:

  • You must own the business or be authorized to represent it. Impersonation, fake or duplicate profiles and misrepresented ownership are prohibited.
  • No ad account or Enterprise agreement is needed. Existing Enterprise customers and advertisers can also go through their OpenAI representative.
  • Availability depends on location and business type. Eligible businesses can claim an existing listing or add one "where ChatGPT is available and the required verification is supported."
  • Local stores and restaurants can currently claim only an existing U.S. listing. If you run an eligible online business and cannot find yourself, you may see an option to add it.
  • Some categories are held back. OpenAI may decline businesses whose offerings raise safety or integrity concerns, and may hold off on other categories until safeguards are ready. Eligibility can change over time.
  • Business Agents have separate, stricter requirements: eligible U.S. ecommerce or online service businesses with a website that meet the industry rules in the Business Agents Policy. A verified profile alone does not establish agent eligibility.

What this means for Indian brands right now

Reading OpenAI's documents together, an Indian brand's position at launch looks like this:

  • Online D2C and SaaS brands can attempt a claim, since ChatGPT is available in India and online businesses may add a listing. Whether your category and verification path are open is decided in the product, so expect some trial and possibly a wait.
  • Local stores, clinics and restaurants in India cannot claim yet. The rule is "existing U.S. listing" only.
  • Business Agents are U.S. only. An Indian brand selling into the U.S. through a U.S.-eligible business may qualify; an India-only brand does not, today.
  • Product feeds: OpenAI's feed spec says the standard upload "currently targets the US" and that other markets need OpenAI to confirm the integration. A feed for an India-only catalog may not reach Indian shoppers yet.
  • Insights are still useful: if you can claim, mentions, website clicks and discovery topics show how ChatGPT cites your site, wherever the users are.

Practical takeaway: claim if you can, prepare your documents and profile copy even if you cannot, and put most of your effort into the off-site signals that drive recommendations in every market. See also our D2C India and India market guides.

Before You Claim: A Preparation Checklist

Most claim problems in OpenAI's own troubleshooting notes come from mismatches: the wrong account, a name that does not match registration documents, a domain that does not match the feed. Ten minutes of preparation avoids most of them.

  1. Pick the owning ChatGPT account on purpose. Later invitations for teammates and agencies depend on the account that claimed the business, and for a claim made inside a ChatGPT workspace, invitees may need to be members of that workspace. Use a company-controlled account, not a personal login someone may leave with.
  2. Confirm your legal name, registered address and registration or tax number. Persona checks these against official records. Write them down exactly as they appear on your registration documents.
  3. Decide your verification route. If the public phone number or email on your listing goes to someone else, plan to use the website domain (DNS TXT record) or Shopify instead.
  4. Get DNS access lined up. If an agency or IT team manages your domain, ask them in advance to add a TXT record when you send it.
  5. Gather identity documents. OpenAI lists a government-issued ID, business registration information and proof that you are authorized to represent the business.
  6. Draft your profile copy (description, category, delivery and returns details) so it matches your website word for word on facts. More on this below.
  7. Audit how you are described elsewhere. If your name, category or description differ across your site, LinkedIn, marketplaces and directories, fix that first. ChatGPT reads those sources too.

How to Claim Your Business, Step by Step

  1. Open Business Hub. Sign in to ChatGPT and open Business Hub from the sidebar. Search for your business by name.
  2. Select the matching listing and confirm the ChatGPT account you will use to manage it.
  3. Choose a verification method from the ones shown for that listing (methods vary by listing) and follow the prompts.
  4. Wait for claim approval. OpenAI reviews the claim and emails a decision or a request for more information. You can cancel while it is under review.
  5. Complete Persona identity verification to unlock editing, insights, feeds and agents (next section).

When things do not go to plan

  • Cannot receive the code: check which other methods the listing offers. Domain verification lets you prove control without changing the public phone number.
  • Already claimed by someone else: open the profile, use the more options menu and select Dispute ownership, then verify with Persona. OpenAI notes that claiming or disputing does not change the profile's visibility.
  • "Contact support" shown: the listing is locked; request access through OpenAI Support.
  • Enterprise tenant listing: if a listing requires a tenant admin, the designated admin of your ChatGPT Enterprise workspace must start the claim. Your Ads admin or web admin may be a different person.
  • Finding a claim you started: sign in with the same account, open Business Hub, select Business and open the existing profile. For a personal invitation, use its link and the exact email it was sent to. Do not forward personal invitations.

Persona Identity Verification: What It Unlocks and How to Pass It

There are two separate approvals, and teams often stop after the first. The claim proves your connection to the business. Persona, the identity provider OpenAI uses, proves who you are. Until Persona is approved you cannot publish profile changes, view insights, connect a product feed or create an agent, even if you already verified the domain or connected Shopify.

What you can doClaim approvedPersona approvedAgent published
See the business in Business HubYesYesYes
Blue verified badgeNoYesYes
Publish profile changesNoYesYes
View insightsNoYesYes
Connect a product feedNoYesYes
Customers can chat with youNoNoYes (U.S. eligible only)

Getting the legal name right

OpenAI's instruction is to use the legal name on your official registration documents in the legal-name field, and your trading name in a separate trade-name or "doing business as" field if there is one. The registered address and any registration or tax number must match your records too.

Example (illustrative): a skincare brand sells as Leafline but is registered as Leafline Naturals Private Limited. The legal-name field gets "Leafline Naturals Private Limited". "Leafline" goes in the trade-name field if offered. The address is the registered office address, not the warehouse.

For Indian companies, OpenAI has not published a country-specific document list. The documents that usually correspond to its three categories are a director's government ID, the Certificate of Incorporation, your GSTIN if a tax number is asked for, and a board resolution or authorization letter if the person verifying is not a director. Treat this as preparation, not a confirmed requirement: Persona shows what it needs during the flow.

Two irreversible steps to know about. First, if you use Persona to create a new profile, OpenAI's approval publishes it automatically, with no separate publish step. Second, only unpublished drafts can be deleted: OpenAI Support cannot delete, unpublish or hide a published profile as part of a setup or ownership request. Get the details right before you submit.

If Persona fails or expires, follow the email instructions and resubmit corrected information if Try again is available. Submitting to Persona does not mean you are approved; features stay locked until the approval email arrives.

Writing Your Profile for a Model, Not a Brochure

After both approvals, you can update your website, business description, product or service category, logo, delivery policy and return policy, and add a cover photo and other optional details. OpenAI reviews and publishes changes, and updates can take time to appear in a listing or an answer.

The description is the field to spend time on. ChatGPT may draw on it when it is relevant, so write it as plain, checkable facts a model can repeat accurately:

  1. What you are in category language a buyer would use.
  2. What you sell, with your main product lines or service types.
  3. Who it is for and where you serve or ship.
  4. Verifiable proof: certifications, years operating, notable facts you can link to.
Weak: "India's most loved, revolutionary skincare brand transforming millions of lives."
Stronger: "Leafline makes fragrance-free sunscreens and moisturisers for oily and acne-prone skin. Products are dermatologically tested, priced between ₹349 and ₹899, and ship across India. Founded in 2021 in Bengaluru."

What the policy does not allow

The Businesses in ChatGPT policy requires information to be truthful and current. It specifically prohibits false or misleading claims about prices, availability, qualifications or likely results; fabricated reviews or endorsements; unsupported guarantees; claims that hide fees or conditions; using photos or logos you lack rights to; and links that do not lead where they say. OpenAI may decline content, limit profile management or remove a profile, and businesses remain responsible for what they submit even though OpenAI does not verify every detail.

Two practical rules follow. Keep delivery and returns settings identical to your website, because a mismatch creates two conflicting "facts". And remember the profile is one source among many; if an outdated directory or review page says something different, fix it there too. Our guide on correcting what AI says about your product covers that process.

Product Feeds, Field by Field

If you sell products, you can connect a feed so ChatGPT has your prices and availability. There are three routes: a Shopify connection, a CSV file upload that follows OpenAI's product feed specification, or a hosted feed URL that opens the file itself. OpenAI notes that eligible Shopify products are already integrated into ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog, and that connecting a feed "doesn't publish an agent or guarantee that ChatGPT recommends your products."

The 11 required fields

OpenAI's developer spec asks for one row per purchasable item or variant, with nine basic fields plus two geo fields:

FieldWhat it must containCommon mistake
item_idStable unique ID per item or variant; never reused for a different itemRegenerating IDs on every export
titleProduct name including the selected variant (aim for 150 characters or less)Keyword-stuffed titles
descriptionFactual plain-text description (aim for 5,000 characters or less)Marketing slogans with no specs
urlStable product page, with the variant selected when possibleURLs on a domain different from the claimed profile
brandBrand as shown on the product pagePlaceholder values
seller_nameName of the seller supplying the offerLeaving it blank on marketplace rows
image_urlDirect image URL (JPEG or PNG) showing this variantLinking to a gallery page instead of an image
availabilityin_stock, out_of_stock, pre_order, backorder or unknownEmpty values, which reject the row

target_countriesUppercase ISO country codes configured for your feedAssuming empty means worldwide
store_countryCountry of the seller's storeUsing it as a regional price override

Rules that quietly decide data quality

  • Variants: each variant gets its own row with a distinct item_id, a shared group_id, listing_has_variations=true and a variant_dict such as {"color":"Black","size":"10"}.
  • Sale prices: sale_price must be above zero, strictly below price, in the same currency. Update the feed when a sale starts or ends; dates in the feed do not schedule changes.
  • Returns: accepts_returns, return_deadline_in_days and a return_policy URL are separate fields. A policy link does not set the boolean. For final-sale items, send accepts_returns=false.
  • Reviews: review_count and star_rating (0 to 5, two decimals) must describe the same review population. Omit a rating when there are no reviews.
  • Unknowns: omit unknown optional values. Do not write placeholder strings like "null" or "n/a"; an empty value does not mean zero or false.
  • Search eligibility: is_eligible_search defaults to true. OpenAI states that eligibility does not guarantee display.
  • Ads and checkout are separate: is_ads_eligible is off unless set, and checkout needs a separately enabled integration.

Why uploads fail

OpenAI's troubleshooting points to three causes: a product URL on a domain that does not match the profile's website, Persona verification still pending, and a missing or wrong DNS TXT record when the claim offers domain verification. Check the upload's Issues view after the first import, and keep a hosted feed URL working so future changes import automatically.

For D2C context on how ChatGPT picks products and merchants, OpenAI's shopping documentation says it weighs structured metadata from first-party and third-party providers, reviews and the user's own context, and ranks merchants on factors like availability, price, quality and whether they are the maker or primary seller. Business Agents: What They Can Do, and What They Cannot

When your business appears in an answer, a user can open your profile and, if you have published an agent, start a separate conversation with it. OpenAI describes that chat as clearly labeled, separate from the user's original conversation and distinct from ChatGPT's independent answers.

A Business Agent canA Business Agent cannot
Explain your products, services and policiesComplete purchases, make bookings or check an order
Help compare your options against a user's needsUse your ChatGPT plugin's data or capabilities
Link to relevant pages on your websitePretend to be human, independent, or endorsed by OpenAI
Offer an optional follow-up through an approved featurePressure users, create false urgency or keep pushing after a no
Describe your business in your own voiceInvent prices, reviews, guarantees or results
Give general informationGive advice that legally requires a professional license

The Business Agents Policy also bars asking for unnecessary or high-risk personal data (health details, government IDs, financial or login credentials), sharing user information or submitting forms without explicit authorization through an approved feature, and instructing the agent to downplay a user's safety concern.

Building one

  1. Confirm eligibility: both approvals done, U.S. ecommerce or online service business, a website, and an eligible industry.
  2. Open your business in the Business tab. Shopify merchants may find a draft agent already created.
  3. Review the information the agent will use, such as product details and the return policy.
  4. Write instructions that follow the policy: tone, when to ask clarifying questions, what to say when it does not know.
  5. Preview with real customer questions and fix the source information when an answer is wrong.
  6. Publish. Customers can only chat with a published agent.

A ten-question test set before you publish

OpenAI suggests previewing with common questions. We recommend a fixed set you rerun after every change:

  1. Can I return an item I bought on sale?
  2. Which of your products suits [specific need]?
  3. What is the difference between [product A] and [product B]?
  4. Do you ship to [location], and how long does it take?
  5. Is this in stock in [size or variant]?
  6. Are you cheaper than [competitor]? (the agent should not invent comparisons)
  7. Will you launch [unannounced product] next month? (it should say there is no confirmed date)
  8. Is this safe if I have [medical condition]? (it should not give licensed advice)
  9. Can you place the order for me? (it should link to your site)
  10. Are you a real person? (it should say it is an AI representing the business)

Sponsored Business Agents and ChatGPT Ads

Businesses can also use an agent as the destination of a ChatGPT ad. OpenAI's Ads Manager documentation describes how this works today:

  • Separate builder: sponsored agents are built in Agent builder inside Ads Manager, rolling out to eligible ad accounts. Settings do not yet sync with an agent built in Business Hub; you reuse the information manually.
  • Campaign setting: the agent attaches to a campaign with the Conversion optimization objective through a setting called Enable agent chats. Users then see Start chat and Visit site.
  • Billing: no separate charge per conversation or message; the campaign's billing applies.
  • Data: advertisers get impressions, clicks and, where available, patterns across conversations, with deeper exploration once an agent passes 25 conversations. No transcripts.
  • Separation: OpenAI says ads do not influence ChatGPT's answers and sponsored placements are labeled.

Whether ads make sense is a separate decision from claiming. Our ads vs organic citations framework and campaign setup guide cover that choice.

Insights in Brief

After both approvals, the Insights view shows aggregated activity over the past 28 days: mentions, website clicks and discovery topics. For an online business, a mention counts only when ChatGPT references your website; naming the brand alone does not count. For a local business, a mention counts when ChatGPT shows a result for that location. Businesses never see individual prompts or conversations, including chats with their own agent, and a blank metric does not necessarily mean zero.

These definitions change how you should read the numbers. We wrote a full guide to reading Business Hub Insights and turning them into a content plan.

Teams, Agencies and Multi-Brand Companies

  • The admin who claimed the business can add teammates and grant an agency access to manage the business or its agent. Each person uses their own ChatGPT account.
  • Each distinct brand has its own profile, even within one company. There is no parent profile that manages every brand or automatically claims its locations.
  • For brands on a managed claim process, location profiles stay locked by default. Ask your OpenAI account contact about the supported path before anyone self-serves.

Eight Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Claiming from a personal account that later leaves the company with access.
  2. Stopping after the claim and never completing Persona, so nothing is editable or measurable.
  3. Creating a new profile through Persona with unchecked details, which publishes on approval and cannot be deleted.
  4. Using the brand name in the legal-name field instead of the registered entity name.
  5. Writing marketing claims into the description that the policy treats as unsupported guarantees.
  6. Uploading a feed with URLs on another domain (a marketplace, a staging site) than the claimed profile.
  7. Reporting "mentions" as "AI visibility" when they only count website references in ChatGPT.
  8. Assuming the profile fixes recommendations and pausing the off-site work that actually moves them.

What Still Decides Whether ChatGPT Recommends You

OpenAI is consistent across every document: the profile is your information, and ChatGPT's recommendations stay independent. In our tested queries, the brands that ChatGPT tends to recommend share a pattern that has nothing to do with a dashboard: they are discussed in the places ChatGPT reads, in language that matches how buyers ask. That usually means:

  • Third-party reviews and comparison pages that mention you with specifics (why social proof matters).
  • Community discussion on forums and Reddit-style threads (what AI uses to recommend brands).
  • Your own pages answering buyer questions directly, with clean structure and schema (schema for AI visibility).
  • A consistent entity: the same name, category and facts everywhere (entity building).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is ChatGPT Business Hub?

Business Hub is the dashboard inside ChatGPT where a business owner or authorized representative claims the business, updates its profile, connects a product feed, views aggregated insights and, if eligible, builds a Business Agent. It is part of Businesses in ChatGPT, a beta OpenAI announced on November 10, 2026.

Is claiming a business in ChatGPT free?

You do not need an Ads account or a ChatGPT Enterprise agreement to claim an eligible business. Sponsoring a Business Agent through ChatGPT Ads is a separate, paid option.

Will claiming make ChatGPT recommend my business more often?

Not by itself. OpenAI says claiming does not guarantee increased visibility or give direct control over ChatGPT's independent recommendations, and that advertising does not influence those answers.

Can Indian brands claim a business in ChatGPT?

Eligible online businesses where ChatGPT is available can attempt a claim, subject to supported verification and business type. Local stores and restaurants can currently claim only existing U.S. listings, Business Agents are U.S. only, and the standard product feed upload currently targets the U.S.

What documents does Persona verification need?

OpenAI says you may need a government-issued ID, business registration information and proof that you are authorized to represent the business. Names, addresses and registration or tax numbers must match your official records.

Can I delete a published profile?

No. Only unpublished drafts can be deleted, and OpenAI Support cannot delete, unpublish or hide a published profile as part of a setup or ownership request.

Do I need Shopify?

No. Shopify speeds up verification and feeds, but any eligible business can claim, upload a CSV feed or use a hosted feed URL, and eligible U.S. businesses can build an agent without Shopify.

Can a Business Agent take orders or bookings?

No. It explains products and policies and links to your website, where customers complete purchases or bookings. It cannot check an order.

Why have my profile changes not appeared yet?

OpenAI reviews and publishes changes, updates can take time to show in a listing or an answer, and ChatGPT may also draw on other sources.
