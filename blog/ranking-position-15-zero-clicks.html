You Are Ranking Position 15 With 10,000 Impressions. Here Is Why You Get Zero Clicks.

Three converging forces kill organic CTR at positions 10 to 20. Two of them are structural shifts you cannot outrank your way out of. Here is the diagnosis and the fix for each one.

This article breaks down the three forces that converge to produce near-zero CTR at positions 10 to 20: AI Overview interception, meta title truncation, and AI search migration. It covers how to identify which forces are affecting your specific pages and what interventions work for each.

10,000 monthly impressions sounds like progress. If you rank position 15 for a query with meaningful search volume, you have real visibility. Someone is choosing your page to show users. The algorithm decided your content is relevant.

And yet: 137 clicks. Maybe 80. Sometimes fewer.

This is not a coincidence or bad luck. It is the predictable output of three specific forces that all hit hardest at positions 10 to 20. Understanding each one separates the fixable problems (one of them is genuinely easy) from the structural shifts that require a different approach entirely.

What the CTR Math Looks Like at Position 15

Before AI Overviews, Backlinko's large-scale CTR analysis found that position 15 in Google delivered approximately 0.8 to 1.1 percent click-through rate. With 10,000 monthly impressions, that is 80 to 110 clicks. Weak, but detectable.

With AI Overview interception active on informational queries, that effective CTR drops to 0.2 to 0.5 percent. 10,000 impressions becomes 20 to 50 clicks. With meta title truncation compounding on top, some pages are seeing under 10 clicks from the same impression volume.

This math explains why so many B2B SaaS founders feel like their organic channel is broken. It is not broken in the traditional sense. It is subject to three converging forces that were not part of the landscape 18 months ago.

The Three Forces

1
Google AI Overviews Intercept Before the Click
Structural

AI Overviews appear above organic results for 15 to 25 percent of all Google searches in 2026, with much higher prevalence for informational and comparison queries. A user searching "how to track AI citations for my SaaS brand" sees a generated summary above all 10 blue links. Most read the summary and stop. Your position 15 result is technically visible, but functionally invisible.

2
Meta Title Truncation Kills CTR on the Pages That Do Surface
Fixable Today

Google truncates page titles at approximately 50 to 60 characters (pixel-width dependent). A title like "How to Structure a GEO Program for a Remote B2B SaaS Marketing Team in 2026" shows as "How to Structure a GEO Program for a Remote B2B SaaS..." in search results. The truncation makes the page purpose ambiguous and signals poor quality to users scanning quickly. A complete, clearly-named title at the same position consistently outperforms a truncated one in CTR tests.

3
Buyers Research in AI Search Tools That Bypass Google Entirely
Structural

When a buyer opens ChatGPT and types a research question about your category, no Google impression is generated. Your GSC sees zero impressions for that session. Your position 15 ranking is irrelevant because the search channel itself was skipped. The buyer forms vendor opinions based on what the AI responds, which is driven by AI citation presence, not Google rank. If your brand is absent from those AI responses, you lost the deal before you knew you were in it.

Which force is dominant for your pages? If your CTR is low but AIOs are absent for your target queries, force 2 (meta title truncation) is the most likely primary cause. If AIOs are present, force 1 is dominant. Force 3 is harder to detect because it generates no GSC signal at all. The only way to measure it is by tracking AI Share of Voice separately from organic traffic.

How to Diagnose Which Force Is Hitting You

Step 1: Pull the impression-to-click discrepancy list

In Google Search Console, go to Search Results. Filter for queries with more than 200 monthly impressions and CTR under 1.5 percent. Sort by impressions descending. This is your AIO and truncation suspect list. Export it.

Step 2: Manual AIO audit

Take the top 20 queries from that list. Search each one in Google in an incognito browser. Mark which ones trigger an AI Overview. Queries with an AIO at positions 5 to 20 are confirmed AIO-intercepted. This is force 1.

Step 3: Meta title audit

For queries without AIOs that still show sub-1.5% CTR, pull the page's title tag. Count the characters. Any title over 58 characters is at risk of truncation. Search the query manually and confirm whether your title appears complete or truncated in the result. This is force 2.

Step 4: AI share of voice audit

Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini. Ask the five most commercially relevant questions a buyer in your category would ask. Count how many responses mention your brand. If your brand appears in zero or one response across 15 queries, force 3 (AI search migration) is actively costing you awareness. The Fix for Each Force

Fix for Force 1: AIO interception

You have two paths. Path A is to push the page to position 1 through 3. Google sometimes cites top-ranked pages inside the AIO itself, which recovers partial click value. This requires significant content and link investment and is not guaranteed. Path B is to restructure the page as answer-engine-ready content and accept that the query's click value has declined, while building AI citation presence for the same query so your brand appears inside the AI response itself.

Path B is the better long-term investment for most B2B SaaS brands. Understanding the difference between GEO and SEO strategy clarifies when to pursue each.

Fix for Force 2: Meta title truncation

This is the easiest fix and should be done immediately. Rewrite every title over 58 characters to be complete and specific within 55 to 60 characters, always include the brand name as a suffix, and make the title answerable as a standalone question without clicking.

Good: "GEO for B2B SaaS: 5-Step Implementation Guide | Jeevan AI"

Bad: "The Complete Step-by-Step Guide to Implementing Generative Engine Optimization for B2B SaaS Companies in..."

Pages with fixed meta titles typically see measurable CTR improvement within 2 to 4 weeks as Google re-indexes the updated titles. This single change has moved CTR by 30 to 80 percent on pages that were already ranking well but truncating.

Fix for Force 3: AI search migration

This one requires the most strategic shift. The buyers who have migrated to AI search tools are forming opinions about vendors through AI-generated responses. If your brand is absent, you need to build AI citation presence through the mechanisms that drive AI recommendations: third-party review platforms, earned media coverage, consistent brand entity documentation, and structured content that answers comparison queries directly.

See how to become a source that AI wants to cite for the tactical breakdown. For measuring your progress, the AI visibility metrics and KPIs guide covers what to track and at what cadence.

Priority order: Fix meta title truncation first (one day of work, immediate impact). Then identify and respond to AIO-intercepted queries. Then build AI citation presence as the long-term position. The order matters because force 2 is purely fixable today, while forces 1 and 3 require sustained investment.

Why This Problem Gets Worse Before It Gets Better

Google is expanding AI Overviews to more query types in each update. The percentage of searches intercepted before organic results will continue to grow through 2026 and 2027. The brands that build AI citation presence now are establishing positions that will become more valuable as the interception rate rises.

A brand ranking position 15 with 10,000 impressions and 130 clicks today will likely see 60 to 70 clicks from the same position and impression volume in 12 months if AIO prevalence doubles on its target queries. The trajectory is clearly down if nothing changes on the AI visibility side.

The good news: Google rankings being fine while you are losing deals is a solvable problem once the mechanism is understood. The brands that address it now will be the ones with durable mid-funnel visibility when the dust settles on the AI search transition.

Separate the Fixable from the Structural

If you have one hour today, audit your meta titles. That is the most leverage per unit of time available in this entire situation. Fix every title over 58 characters. Watch CTR move within weeks.

If you have one quarter, build a systematic AI citation presence. Start with third-party reviews on G2 and Capterra, then earned media, then structured brand entity documentation. This is what determines whether your brand exists in the minds of buyers who never touch a Google search result.

The position 15 problem is not a permanent ceiling. It is a signal that two channels need attention: the truncated interface between your pages and Google's users, and the AI-mediated channel where buyers are forming vendor opinions before they ever click anything at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do I get thousands of GSC impressions but almost no clicks?

There are three main causes. First, Google AI Overviews are answering queries above your listing so users do not need to click through. Second, your meta title may be truncated at 50 to 60 characters, which reduces click-through rate by making the page purpose unclear. Third, some buyers in your category are now using ChatGPT or Perplexity for research instead of Google, meaning those searches never generate an impression in your GSC at all. The most common combination is AIO interception plus meta title truncation, which can reduce an already low position-15 CTR to near zero.

What CTR can I expect at position 15 in 2026?

Average CTR at positions 13 to 17 ranges from 0.5 to 1.4 percent according to large-scale CTR studies. For queries where an AI Overview appears, that CTR drops by an estimated 40 to 64 percent, bringing effective CTR to 0.2 to 0.8 percent. With 10,000 monthly impressions, this translates to 20 to 80 clicks per month. If you are seeing fewer than 20 clicks from 10,000 impressions, you have AIO interception plus likely a truncated or ambiguous meta title.

Should I try to rank position 1 to escape the AIO problem?

Moving to positions 1 through 3 helps but does not fully solve the problem. Google sometimes cites pages inside the AI Overview itself, which restores some click value for top-ranking pages. However, not all top-ranked pages get cited in AIOs, and for informational queries with mature AIOs, even position 1 can lose 20 to 40 percent of its typical CTR. The more durable fix is to build AI citation presence outside of Google so that regardless of what happens to Google CTR, your brand remains visible in AI-mediated research.

How do I know if an AI Overview is intercepting my queries?

The fastest check is manual. Take your top 20 queries from GSC by impression volume and search for each one in Google in an incognito browser. Note which ones trigger an AI Overview. Any query with an AIO and your ranking at positions 5 to 20 is likely losing most of its potential clicks to the AIO. In GSC, these queries will show high impressions and CTR below 1.5 percent at position 8 to 20. Cross-referencing the two views gives you a reliable AIO interception list.
